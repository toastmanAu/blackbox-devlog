Fortnightly #6: BlackBox confirms its own sales on-chain

2026-07-22 · wave-2firmwarehardware

The V2 prototype has been taking test payments for a while now — but the payment screen was working off a stand-in. If the customer’s phone reached the device over Wi-Fi, the sale was treated as paid; the actual settlement was verified out-of-band. This fortnight that placeholder came out, and the device started confirming sales against the chain itself. The two payments below are the first proofs of the new path, not the first payments.

Wave 7b — the peripherals come up

The two UART peripherals that make a point-of-sale device a point-of-sale device are alive on real hardware. A GM861S barcode scanner runs on UART2 behind a non-blocking driver (v0.2.6 init sequence, 9600 → 115200 bootstrap, ACK-strip and idle-gap framing), wired into the sale flow so scanning a product looks it up in inventory_svc and drops it straight into the cart. A QR204 thermal printer on UART1 prints a real receipt — line items, totals, a nervos: payment URI and a live date line from a new SNTP clock_svc.

Several fixes only a bench session could have found. On-screen QR and barcodes were being rendered inverted, which decoders reject outright — they needed black-on-white with proper quiet zones. The indexed canvases were triggering a ~130 KB-per-frame decode allocation behind an intermittent QR failure, fixed by flattening to RGB565. The device Wi-Fi build turned out to be compiling the simulator branch, because an __has_include seam defeated library discovery; the real stack costs another 614 KB and needed a 2.5 MB app partition. And the payment URI scheme was corrected to the one JoyID actually implements.

Wave 8 — on-chain confirmation

The proxy’s replacement is three pieces. ckb_addr decodes a bech32m address to a lock script on-device, host-tested with round-trip vectors. chain_svc speaks get_cells_capacity JSON-RPC to a CKB indexer node. confirm_svc runs a FreeRTOS poll task that arms when the QR screen appears and auto-advances to Success when the merchant lock’s capacity rises by the sale amount.

The interesting work was concurrency, not RPC. Review caught a dual-task race in the poll design that could leak a task and produce a false CONFIRMED — unacceptable on a money path — fixed with a generation counter and per-task context, plus a critical section closing a TOCTOU between the generation check and the state write. Disarming no longer blocks LVGL either.

Then the checkpoint: two payments auto-detected on real hardware, with a receipt printed. The confirming transaction hash is now captured at CONFIRMED and shown on the success screen and receipt — the first step toward per-tx matching. Getting there also meant standing up chain infrastructure: an OrangePi-3B testnet indexer node, bootstrapped from an existing RocksDB rather than syncing from genesis, since the old node was decommissioned.

Waves 9a & 9b — a keyless merchant wallet

A merchant needs to send, not just receive: refunds, and moving takings somewhere safer than the till. But a shop-floor device should never hold a key.

The answer is the Agent Gateway from the Vault companion wallet. The POS holds only a scoped biscuit token, provisioned by scanning a QR bundle the wallet now emits, and submits send_ckb intents to the relay over TLS. The phone re-validates against policy and signs. Wave 9a built that path plus a Refund flow and a result screen; Wave 9b added an async takings balance for the merchant lock and a Sweep screen that moves CKB to a persisted cold-storage address.

Review earned its keep again — corrupt-token parsing, a wrong-payee hazard from a stale scanned recipient, a send-path mux gap, and a provisioning bundle silently truncated by a 200-byte scan buffer (the biscuit bundles run 700–1000 chars) were all caught before hardware. The build sits at 61% flash / 34% RAM.
