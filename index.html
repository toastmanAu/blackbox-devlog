blackbox devlog

Dev log for the BlackBox CKB point-of-sale device. Wave 2.

Fortnightly #11: the terminal checks the chain itself

2026-11-12 · wave-2firmware

The terminal now runs a CKB light client and confirms sales itself, against any public node, with the phone kept as a fallback. Twenty bench sales out of twenty confirmed, then a mainnet sale. Alongside: the link to the Vault is pinned to a key carried in the pairing QR, staff keys get a PIN, the terminal keeps a usage log for the Kenya trial, and two bench hangs turn out to be a receipt printer and a 32-bit timer.

Fortnightly #10: the terminal learns to take a card

2026-10-22 · wave-2firmwarehardware

A third way to pay lands: prepaid NFC cards that carry their own balance, debited card-side on the tap with the network unplugged, then batched to the Vault which settles them on chain. The money path is built to get a tap wrong safely — every debit is journaled before the card is written, and 'landed', 'didn't land' and 'unknown' are told apart. Alongside: item vouchers backed by tiny chain cells, staff keys authenticated by a tag's NXP originality signature, barcode scan-to-enter in inventory, and a UI freeze traced to a flash write on the wrong CPU core.

Fortnightly #9: Bitcoin becomes a second rail, and BlackBox gets a board of its own

2026-09-21 · wave-2hardwarefirmware

The VIEWE S3 board comes up and resolves a real funding block against the testnet indexer; the light client gets an explicit answer to where its scan starts, with no silent defaults. Bitcoin lands as a second receive rail — receive-only, keyless, watch-only from a merchant xpub, with SEEN and VERIFIED as separate states and VERIFIED meaning a Merkle proof checked against a header chain the terminal validated itself. And a custom 4-layer PCB now exists in KiCad, generated from a source model rather than drawn, with six blockers still open before anything goes to fab.

Fortnightly #8: v0.3.0 ships, and BlackBox moves to new silicon

2026-08-28 · wave-2hardwarefirmware

v0.3.0 goes out as a release the fielded prototype's own one-press updater collects, repartitioning its flash with no cable attached. Then the board changes: a 1.68 MB app in a 2 MB slot left nowhere to put the light client’s ~726 KB, and the V1 flash was allocated end to end, so v1.0.0 forks to a VIEWE ESP32-S3 — 16 MB flash, 8 MB octal PSRAM, an ST7796 panel and a touch controller LovyanGFX gets wrong — landing at 25% of a slot three times larger. A dependency graph overturns the ESP-IDF migration the light client was supposed to require, vendor documentation proves wrong in two load-bearing places, three brick-class traps are written down before any wiring, headers and looms go on the bench, and the repo gets its first CI — which immediately finds the simulator had not built for two months.

Fortnightly #7: no third machine, pricing returns, and updates over the air

2026-08-06 · wave-2firmwarehardware

The Tier-1 arc lands: a V1 device needs nothing but the companion wallet app — signing over LAN mDNS/TLS, chain reads proxied through the phone, hardware-verified end-to-end. Live pricing returns to the rewrite with a hardened Binance+FX rate chain and six currencies, a review catch saves half of all sales from never confirming, and a permanent updater partition brings browser OTA plus a no-cable migration path for v0.2.6 units.

Fortnightly #6: BlackBox confirms its own sales on-chain

2026-07-22 · wave-2firmwarehardware

The V2 prototype's payment path graduates from a Wi-Fi stand-in to real on-chain confirmation, passing its hardware checkpoint with two auto-detected sales and a printed receipt. Scanner and thermal printer come up on real UARTs, and the POS gains a keyless merchant wallet with refunds and cold-storage sweep.

Fortnightly #5: The POS app comes alive on LVGL

2026-06-30 · wave-2firmwareui

BlackBox's on-device POS app takes shape on LVGL — payments, QR, full inventory CRUD and a sales cart/invoice flow on a new ILI9488 display target; the Vault companion wallet adds an on-chain Agent Gateway and embedded light client, and the board emulator goes agentic.

Fortnightly #4: BlackBox Vault companion wallet goes on-chain

2026-06-15 · wave-2wallettooling

BlackBox Vault — the native Android companion wallet — ships Wave 2 with Nervos DAO, real on-chain ML-DSA-65 post-quantum sends, and biometric security gates; the board emulator gains thermal printer and QR-scanner peripherals.

2026-05-28 · wave-2toolingui

Open-source ESP32 board emulator shrinks the embedded debug loop, BlackBox is being ported to LVGL on top of it, and a new post-quantum messaging protocol (cemp-pq) lands for the backend.
