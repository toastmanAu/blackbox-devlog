Fortnightly #7: no third machine, pricing returns, and updates over the air

2026-08-06 · wave-2firmwarehardware

The last fortnight closed out three arcs that each would have headlined a quieter cycle: the device lost its last server dependency, got its live prices back, and learned to update itself.

No third machine

The two-tier transport work finished and passed its hardware checkpoint: a Tier-1 device pairs with the merchant’s phone by QR, resolves it over mDNS, and does everything through it — refund and sweep signing via the relay wire contract served locally over self-signed TLS, and sale confirmation plus balance through a chain-read proxy on the phone. No relay, no indexer, no Tailscale. Six device-only bugs fell out of the live checkpoint (the Android HTTP server that couldn’t actually serve HTTPS was the best of them), the board’s forgotten 4 MB of PSRAM got switched on, and receipts picked up the BlackBox logo — after a detour through the QR204’s raster quirks — plus a printed refund receipt.

Pricing returns, hardened

The original prototype always priced sales live; the LVGL rewrite had been running on a hardcoded stub since the port began. That gap closed this fortnight, with more armour than before: a background task feeds the POS from Binance’s CKB ticker crossed with a daily USD→fiat table, falling back to CoinGecko with exponential backoff when rate-limited. Merchants pick from six display currencies, quotes lock their rate the moment they’re struck, and a stale feed blocks new quotes rather than guessing — tapping checkout kicks an immediate refetch. The fortnight’s best save came from review, not hardware: the payment QR rounded CKB to two decimals while the chain watcher demanded full precision, so at any real rate roughly half of paid sales would have sat unconfirmed forever. One quantization at quote-strike now keeps the QR, the watcher and the receipt in exact agreement.

Updates over the air

v0.3.0 ships with a permanent factory updater partition: tap Update in Settings, the device reboots into a 1 MB updater that joins Wi-Fi (or opens its own AP), and a browser on the LAN uploads new firmware and assets — with the bootloader falling back to the updater if an upload is bad. Fielded v0.2.6 units get there with no cable: their existing web-OTA delivers the updater, which repartitions the flash in place and comes up ready to receive v0.3.0. The review process earned its keep here — four distinct brick vectors (a cross-core cache race during the table write among them) were caught and fixed before any hardware was risked.

Next: the hardware checkpoint for OTA and migration, golden-image recaptures, and then the long-promised Fiber instant-settlement path.
